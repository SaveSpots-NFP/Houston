## 6.3 Analysis Plan

**Status: draft for Ali and Sameer review. No pipeline code should begin until both approve this plan.**

1. **Environment.** Houston/Harris County is Environment B with a request-based hybrid. Public HCPH/HCIFS aggregate mortality and Texas DSHS death-certificate data exist; HCIFS offers limited recent case lookup and request routes but no public bulk overdose or toxicology extract was identified. The analysis proceeds with privacy-preserving aggregate sources; any response to a future request changes this classification only after access, definitions, and permissions are documented.

2. **Primary measured quantity.** The primary directly measured quantity will be the proportion of candidate fixed naloxone access outlets that a dated verification audit confirms are publicly accessible and actually dispensing naloxone during stated hours. The audit will record outlet-level operational attributes only, not people or overdose events; public outputs will aggregate results to the approved geography. This measures realized access conditions more directly than relying on a published directory alone. The count/location inventory is measured; modeled resident access distances remain separately labeled modeled. If the team cannot complete a defensible audit, this primary quantity must be reapproved rather than quietly replaced with an entirely published or modeled statistic.

3. **Three research questions.** (a) Where is burden? Describe HCPH substance-involved mortality and published nonfatal visit patterns at the finest geography each source supports. (b) Where is the service gap? Compare those burden indicators with verified naloxone access, treatment supply, transit access, and contextual barriers without conflating need, access, and utilization. (c) Where should supply go? Identify aggregate areas where burden and access barriers overlap, then test whether priority rankings remain stable under disclosed weights; recommendations are planning priorities, not causal estimates.

4. **Finest defensible geography.** Use HCPH's documented ZIP geography for the local mortality comparison if a reproducible export is obtained; otherwise report mortality at county level. Census tracts may support ACS/SVI context and may be aggregated to ZIP only through an explicit, population-weighted crosswalk labeled modeled. Never allocate mortality from county or ZIP to census blocks, facilities, or points. Keep service-audit records internal at outlet level and publish only safely aggregated summaries.

5. **Composite layers and sensitivity.** Candidate domains: burden (published HCPH mortality and ESSENCE rates, kept distinct); service gap (measured verified naloxone access and, only after acquisition, modeled transit access and documented treatment supply); context (published ACS poverty/uninsurance and CDC SVI). Provisional domain weights are burden 40%, service gap 35%, context 25%, with equal weights within each domain. Sensitivity variants: 50/30/20 and 30/45/25. Report rank correlation, overlap among highest-priority areas, and rank changes. Exclude unavailable inputs rather than impute them; final weights and scaling must be approved before code.

6. **Non-computable metrics.** Current public data cannot identify individual histories, exact overdose/decedent locations, case-level toxicology, causal effects of new supply, comprehensive naloxone coverage, or population-level outreach reach. Local polysubstance share, HCV burden by transmission category, treatment retention beyond the named program outcome, and nonfatal unique-person counts remain gaps until definitions and aggregates are obtained. Report each gap and its consequence; do not infer or downscale it.
